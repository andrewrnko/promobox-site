Spot graphicsor full wrap?

Nothing, spot graphics, a partial wrap or a full wrap. What each level does, what it costs to start, and how to pick.

Every wrap job starts with one question. How much of the vehicle should we cover? There are four honest answers: nothing, spot graphics, a partial wrap, or a full wrap. Here is what each one does and who it fits.

Start with the white van

A plain white van is not wasted space. White paint is a clean background. A dark logo, a big service line and a phone number on white can read from across a parking lot. Spot graphics use that. You pay for the letters and panels, not for covering paint that is already doing a job.

The catch is design. Spot graphics only work when they look planned. Random decals on every panel read like three different companies did the work. Repeat one color, line things up, and give the business name the most room.

What partial and full add

A partial wrap prints color on part of the vehicle, often the back half to two-thirds, and blends into the paint. You get a big color field and a strong look with less film and less install time than a full wrap.

A full wrap covers every painted panel. It gives the most room for color, photos and a message, and the paint color stops mattering. It also costs the most, because it uses the most film and the most install work.

What do you need the vehicle to do?

Shop note

Ask for two layouts before you decide. One that uses the paint you have, one with more coverage. Look at both shrunk down, the way a stranger at a stoplight would see them.

What people miss

Coverage is not the message. A full wrap with a paragraph of services on it reads worse than a door with three clear lines. Whatever level you pick, the vehicle should answer three questions fast. Who are you? What do you do? How do I reach you?

Hardware matters too. Door handles, rear door seams and badges cut through artwork. A phone number split by a seam is harder to read. Send photos of both sides and the rear so the layout is built around the real vehicle, not a flat template.

And plan for the next vehicle. If a second van is coming next year, pick a layout that works on both. We document fleet layouts, colors and placement, so the second one matches the first.
