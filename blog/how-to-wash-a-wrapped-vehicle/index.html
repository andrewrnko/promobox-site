Wrapped van?Change the wash.

A wrap needs a different wash than paint. Hand wash, keep the pressure washer off the edges, and skip the wax on matte.

A new wrap changes how you should wash the vehicle. Paint can take a lot of abuse. Film has edges, seams and a laminate that wears. A few habits keep it looking new for years.

Start with the film's care guide

Ask which film went on your vehicle and keep its care sheet. Wrap films and finishes have different cleaning limits. Gloss, satin and matte do not all like the same products. When in doubt, the film maker's guide wins over anything you read online, including this.

The basic wash

  1. 1

    Rinse first

    Rinse off loose dirt and grit before you touch the film. Grit under a mitt scratches.

  2. 2

    Hand wash

    Use a soft mitt and a mild car wash soap. Work top down.

  3. 3

    Go easy on edges

    Wipe along edges, not into them. Do not scrub at a seam.

  4. 4

    Rinse again

    Get all the soap off. Dried soap leaves marks.

  5. 5

    Dry it

    Use a soft microfiber towel or let it air dry out of direct sun.

Pressure washers and car washes

A pressure washer is fine if you are careful. Keep the nozzle back from the film, keep the spray moving, and never aim it straight at an edge or seam. Follow the pressure and distance limits in your film's care guide. A tight jet aimed at a corner is the fastest way to lift one.

Automatic washes with spinning brushes can catch edges and scuff the laminate. Touchless washes are gentler, but strong chemicals can still be hard on film. Hand washing is the safe bet.

Wrap care, quick version

DoSkip

SpillsClean fuel, sap and droppings soonLet them bake on for weeks

Matte is a different finish

A wax or polish that adds shine to paint will add shine to matte film too. That sounds harmless until one panel is shinier than the next. Use cleaners made for matte wraps, and skip anything that promises gloss.

Shop note

Noticed a lift? Photograph the area and send it to us. Do not pick at it, trim it or try to glue it down. Early advice is cheaper than a homemade patch.

Do these few things and the wrap has its best shot at the full five to seven years most quality wraps get with proper care.
